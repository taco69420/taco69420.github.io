## Quick Answer
Sonic stakes send low-frequency vibrations through the soil every 30 seconds or so. Here's the part most pages selling them leave out: UC IPM tested this class of device and frightening devices "haven't been effective in research trials." So this page isn't going to claim they work. If you buy them, treat them as a maybe around a single bed and put the real effort into trapping.

## The Process
1. Identify the active zone by locating [fresh mounds](/problems/mound-id/) and probing for main tunnels.
2. Drive stakes into the soil every 15–20 feet along the active perimeter — not just in the middle of the yard.
3. Make sure the solar panel faces direct sun; shaded stakes lose charge fast and stop pulsing.
4. Leave them running continuously for at least 2 weeks before judging results — and judge honestly against the mound count, not against hope.
5. If new mounds appear outside the staked zone, the gopher moved — reposition stakes to cut off the new run.

## What the Research Says
UC IPM's pest notes are direct about this: "Frightening devices such as vibrating stakes, ultrasonic devices, and wind-powered pinwheels haven't been effective in research trials." *(Source: [Baldwin, R.A. — UC IPM Pest Notes: Pocket Gophers, 2025](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/PMG/PESTNOTES/pn7433.html))*

That's the research, and this page isn't going to argue with it. Moving the stakes around periodically gets suggested as a workaround, but no study backs that either. What actually clears a yard is trapping.

## Bagger Pro-Tip
Think of sonic stakes like a fence you're not sure is switched on. They don't kill anything, and the research says they don't reliably move anything either. ## Safety Warning
No rodenticide involved and no trigger mechanism, which is what makes them unobjectionable in a yard with a dog in it. Two real risks: driving the stake into an irrigation line — probe the soil first if you're near [drip lines](/problems/irrigation-damage/) — and leaving a hard stake at shin height where kids run.
