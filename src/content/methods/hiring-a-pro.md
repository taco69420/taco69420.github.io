## Quick Answer
Most gopher problems are one-animal jobs you can handle yourself with [a pair of traps](/best-gopher-traps/). But there are situations where calling a professional is the right call — large infestations across multiple zones, commercial properties, HOA common areas, or when you've been at it for months with no results. Knowing when to escalate is part of the process.

## When to Call a Pro

- More than 5–6 active [mound](/problems/mound-id/) clusters across different parts of the yard simultaneously
- Recurring pressure despite consistent trapping for 4+ weeks
- Commercial properties, orchards, or large acreage where systematic treatment is needed
- Active gopher activity near foundations, retaining walls, or infrastructure where you need documented treatment records
- You simply don't want to do it yourself — that's a valid reason too

## The Process
1. Get at least two quotes. Gopher control pricing varies wildly by property size and method — I keep the current ranges in the [cost guide](/cost/) rather than repeating them here.
2. Ask specifically what method they use. [Trapping](/methods/victor-traps/) and [CO fumigation](/methods/carbon-monoxide/) are preferred. Be cautious with anyone who defaults immediately to poison bait on a property with kids, pets, or edible gardens.
3. Ask if they guarantee results and what the callback policy is. A reputable operator will come back if new activity appears within the treatment window.
4. Check for a valid [California](/southern-california-gopher-control/) Pest Control Operator license (PCO license) if they're applying any restricted-use products.
5. After treatment, ask for a written summary of what was done — this matters for foundation damage documentation or HOA records.

## Bagger Pro-Tip
A good pro isn't just someone who sets traps. They should be able to read a tunnel system, identify the main runs, and tell you why they're setting where they're setting. If they can't explain their approach, find someone else.

## Safety Warning
Never allow an unlicensed operator to apply fumigants or restricted pesticides on your property. In California, certain products require a licensed applicator. Ask to see their license — a legitimate pro won't hesitate to show it.
